The Synergy Cube

A singleton unpowered cube built on Legacy-level efficiency — fetchlands, shocklands, and clean removal alongside synergy-first archetypes and a splash of flavor crossovers.

About

What is the Synergy Cube?

The Synergy Cube is a singleton cube built on Legacy-level efficiency without the format-warping stuff — no [[Black Lotus]], no Moxen, no [[Ancestral Recall]]. What fills that gap instead is depth: real mana dorks, real mana rocks, a full cycle of fetchlands feeding a full cycle of shocklands, and removal so clean ([[Swords to Plowshares]], [[Path to Exile]], [[Fatal Push]]) that it's rarely a wrong pick.

Sitting alongside the Legacy staples is a run of higher-flavor cards — Final Fantasy, Avatar: The Last Airbender, and Duskmourn crossovers among them — chosen on power level and role, not novelty. They play the same game as everything else here.

The mono-colored sections each contain multiple distinct themes that overlap and play well together — so you're rarely forced on rails, but always rewarded for reading the signals. It drafts best at six players, though we make it work whenever we can scrounge up the headcount.

Design Philosophy

What's Not Here — and Why

This cube has long moved away from the max-power paradigm. The goal is high variance in drafting but low variance in gameplay — multiple branching decision trees, always a way to outplay your opponent. That means a deliberate set of exclusions.

No fast mana
[[Sol Ring]], the Moxen ([[Mox Ruby]], [[Mox Emerald]], etc.), [[Chrome Mox]], [[Mox Diamond]], [[Black Lotus]] — all out. Mana dorks and rocks are how decks get ahead here, not free acceleration.
No absurd card advantage
[[Library of Alexandria]], [[Ancestral Recall]], [[Skullclamp]], [[Timetwister]] — cards that refill a hand for free or near-free collapse the decision space the cube is built around.
No format-warping power
[[Time Walk]], [[Balance]] — a single card that ends the game or resets the board at no real cost isn't interesting, it's just a trap for whoever doesn't have it.
No oppressive or repetitive cards
[[Recurring Nightmare]], [[Opposition]], [[Umezawa's Jitte]], [[Minsc & Boo, Timeless Heroes]], [[Broadside Bombardiers]] — cards that create poor gameplay loops or make it impossible for the opponent to catch up.
No free / pitch spells
[[Force of Will]], [[Daze]], [[Snuff Out]], [[Treachery]] — the emphasis is on reading open mana. Interaction should always cost mana.
Exceptions for proactive effects only: [[Grief]], [[Gitaxian Probe]], [[Mishra's Bauble]], [[Urza's Bauble]], [[Frantic Search]].
No cheat-into-play cards
[[Oath of Druids]], [[Through the Breach]], [[Sneak Attack]], [[Show and Tell]], [[Natural Order]], [[Eureka]] are all out.
Exception: [[Tinker]] stays to push the artifact deck — and there's an abundance of removal for artifact creatures to keep it honest.
No strict combos
No [[Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker]] / [[Splinter Twin]], no Persist combo ([[Anafenza, Kin-Tree Spirit]]). Synergistic pairs are fine; two-card instant wins are not.
Example of the line: [[Reanimate]] is in, but [[Entomb]] is not.
No signets or talismans
[[Boros Signet]], [[Talisman of Conviction]] and their cycle are out. Two-color fixing rocks push three-color decks in a direction that undermines the guild-lane draft structure.
No non-interactive cards
[[True-Name Nemesis]] and similar — there should always be a way to outplay your opponent. Cards that deny all interaction aren't interesting, they just end discussions.
Curated tutors only
No [[Demonic Tutor]] or [[Vampiric Tutor]]. Allowed: [[Green Sun's Zenith]] (toolbox), [[Mystical Tutor]] and [[Tinker]] (sorcery build-arounds). Companions [[Yorion, Sky Nomad]] (blink) and [[Lurrus of the Dream-Den]] (recursion) bolster specific archetypes.
Archetypes

How the Manabase Signals the Lanes

The land base is built lane by lane — every one of the ten two-color pairs gets its own fetchland, shockland, and surveil land, plus a fourth or fifth wildcard land (a horizon land or allied manland depending on the pair). Riding along with those lands are three or four gold cards built to reward staying in exactly that lane.

That combination — a guild's fetch, shock, and surveil land wheeling back around with its signpost legends still in the pack — is the clearest signal an archetype is open.

Synergies

The Cross-Lane Strategies

The more interesting decks usually don't stay inside one lane — they're built around a synergy that cuts across several of them.

Reanimator
The cube's most explosive plan. Fill the yard early with [[Faithless Looting]], [[Careful Study]], and [[Stitcher's Supplier]], then cheat a game-ending threat into play two or three turns ahead of schedule.
[[Reanimate]] · [[Griselbrand]] · [[Archon of Cruelty]] · [[Emrakul, the Promised End]]
Affinity
Five original Mirrodin artifact lands form the backbone of a near-colorless aggro plan. The whole shell barely touches colored mana, making it one of the cube's most consistent strategies.
[[Steel Overseer]] · [[Arcbound Ravager]] · [[Hangarback Walker]]
Humans
A go-wide white tribal plan built around stacking lord effects. [[Champion of the Parish]] and [[Thalia's Lieutenant]] scale with every Human you play, and [[Winota, Joiner of Forces]] bridges into red to reward flooding the board with cheap bodies.
[[Champion of the Parish]] · [[Thalia's Lieutenant]] · [[Winota, Joiner of Forces]]
Lands Matter
[[Life from the Loam]] and [[Crucible of Worlds]] turn the deep fetchland package from a mana-fixing tool into a genuine card-advantage engine. Feeds a long, grindy game plan that's hard to race.
[[Wrenn and Six]] · [[The Gitrog Monster]] · [[Field of the Dead]]
Blink Value
A quieter white shell built around repeating enter-the-battlefield triggers. [[Ephemerate]] and [[Restoration Angel]] reset [[Monastery Mentor]], [[Adeline, Resplendent Cathar]], and [[Sun Titan]] over and over without over-committing.
[[Ephemerate]] · [[Restoration Angel]] · [[Flickerwisp]] · [[Sun Titan]]
Strategy

Drafting It

1
Read the signposts. A guild's fetch, shock, surveil land, and gold legends wheeling back to you is the clearest sign that lane is open.
2
Take the efficient removal. [[Swords to Plowshares]], [[Path to Exile]], and [[Fatal Push]] are safe in almost any deck — take them over a speculative bomb if you're still unsure of your lane.
3
Fast mana is scarce power here, not filler. With no Power Nine, dorks like [[Noble Hierarch]] and rocks like [[Mind Stone]] are how decks actually get ahead — prioritize them if you're building toward the top end.
4
Commit to two colors, splash carefully. The guild lanes are built to support a clean two-color deck; a third color should be earned with fetches, shocks, or a fixing land, not assumed.
5
Watch for the cross-lane packages. Reanimator, Affinity, and Humans all pull pieces from outside a single lane — if those cards are flowing, they're often worth breaking plan for.
Utility Land Draft

The ULD

Lands are arguably the most important part of Magic: The Gathering, and it's a shame that in most draft environments they barely register. The Utility Land Draft fixes that. After the main draft, lands with narrow synergies are drafted rotisserie-style — each player makes picks in turn, no pack, no passing.

4 picks per player
6-player → 24 lands
8-player → 32 lands
~40 cards in the pool

The ULD is not for mana fixing — those lands are already in the main cube. It exists to give archetypes access to lands with narrow synergies: [[Raging Ravine]] for Gruul, [[Creeping Tar Pit]] for Dimir, [[Field of the Dead]] for Lands Matter, and so on. The pool stays small by design; it's a lot to take in for new players and the goal is to celebrate lands as a drafting resource, not to overwhelm.

The main cube also runs a handful of non-singleton utility lands: double fetchlands, double enemy shocklands, triple [[Prismatic Vista]], and triple [[Wasteland]].
