We've been playing Puerto Rico 1897 for over a year and it's time to make some changes to our default setup. I made a few targeted swaps to the building roster. The goals was to create more interesting decisions, increase the varied gameplay, and encourage more unique strategies. Here's what changed and why.

<!--more-->

## The Changes

### Canal (1 db, 1 VP) — replaces Hacienda

**Old text:** Gain 1 Estate from the bag before taking the usual one.

**New text:** Produce 1 additional Crate from Large Fruit Depot and Large Sugar Mill.

Hacienda was a fine card but its effect — grabbing a blind estate before your normal pick — felt low-impact and a bit opaque. I wanted to remove this tile from the beginning, but the other players really liked picking this building. After some time, it almost never gets picked these days, so it's removal was imminent. The Canal takes the same cheap slot and does something more legible: it rewards committing to Fruit or Sugar infrastructure with a direct production bonus. This way, players are incentivized with going through the middle route instead of just the extremes, especially in a game that rewards min/max-ing.

At 1 db it's almost always worth picking up early if you're touching either large production building. The interesting tension is that it does nothing on its own — you need the Large Fruit Depot or Large Sugar Mill manned and running before you see any return. This should push players into production chains sooner rather than hedging with late buys.

---

### Publishing House (8 db, 3 VP) — replaces School

**Old text:** Built Buildings come with 1 Worker from the supply.

**New text:** Double Privilege.

School provided a steady stream of workers as you built, which was quietly strong but easy to ignore. Except for extreme circumstances (like trying to end the game with a large commercial building), no one would choose this. It was too much to enable for only a single play. In addition, since we do play the Monument, that building entirely takes that game play pattern and provides it in a late game building anyway.

The Publishing House replaces the School with something much more dramatic: you get the role privilege twice. Pick Captain → ship twice. Pick Craftsman → produce twice. Pick Trader → trade twice. The effect scales with how well you can time your role selections and how many roles you personally want to trigger. At 8 db it sits alongside Factory and Harbor, and like those buildings it demands you build your strategy around it to see full value. Sharing the same db value is a little troublesome, but now introduces a real inflection point for mid-to-late game buildings; do you want to go for VPs (Harbor), go for money (Factory), or go for pure value?

This changes the calculus on role selection for the whole table. Opponents will see you pick a role and know you're getting double mileage from it, which means they'll start playing around it. Publishing House creates visible, reactive gameplay in a way School never did.

---

### Cathedral (10 db, 4 VP) — replaces Fortress

**Old text:** Score 1 VP for every 3 Workers.

**New text:** Receive bonus 1/3/6/10 VPs for 1/2/3/4 sets of 3 like plantation/forest/quarry tiles on your island.

Fortress rewarded colonist flooding, which was fine but redundant with how workers already function as an economy. Cathedral opens a new end-game axis entirely: land specialization. This is a build-around in every sense of the word. You can get massive VPs for trying to solve a puzzle with your estates.

The bonus scales sharply. Two sets of three matching tiles — six of the same tile type on your island — gives you 3 bonus VP, which is already worth it. Four sets (twelve matching tiles, a completely specialized island) gives you 10 bonus VP on top of the face value. That's potentially the highest single-building score in the game.

In practice, landing four sets is unrealistic without actively drafting toward it, which means Cathedral changes how you think about Settler phases from the start. Do you take the convenient mixed tiles or commit to a theme? That decision ripples into which production buildings you buy and how you prioritize Captain rounds.

It also creates a different kind of endgame tension: other players can see your island and gauge how far you are from a Cathedral payoff, giving them information to race or block.

---

### City Hall removed

City Hall (Score 1 VP per Commercial Building) was removed without a replacement in that slot. It was a fine card but it encouraged building blue buildings purely for the synergy count rather than for their effects, which led to some awkward games where someone was buying Small Markets they didn't intend to use just to pump the score. Removing it cleans up the commercial building space. Not to mention, that trying to build City Hall means that you do not want to ship at all so production buildings won't be built. It was a very steep build-around that didn't reward you correctly as you will have destroyed any versatility you have by not producing or shipping anything. This likely goes along with the corn strategy, but you'd want more cheaper buildings with that path - this isn't it.

## What This Means for Games

The net effect of these changes is a shift toward **commitment**. Canal wants you locked into a production chain. Publishing House wants you to pick roles with intent. Cathedral wants you to specialize your island from the opening Settler.

The old set had more cards that worked passively in the background (Hacienda dripping estates, School dripping workers, Fortress rewarding your natural colonist accumulation). This new set has more cards that ask a question - are you building toward something specific? That is the hallmark of all open sandbox games, reward those for whom specialize and commit the most (and if they can actually pull it off in gameplay).

We'll see how it plays out. I expect Cathedral to either do nothing or warp a game completely — that's usually a sign the design is interesting, even if it only happens once in every 15 games. At a minimum, it will allow the player going for this to prioritize the Planter, which loses a lot of appeal later in the game (so does the Recruiter).
